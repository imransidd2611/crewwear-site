Safety vest classes explained

Owners ask which class of vest they need. The short answer: it depends on where and how your crew works, and the person who sets the rule is your client, employer or state, not the vest seller.

What the classes are

In the US, high visibility apparel is commonly sorted into classes under the ANSI/ISEA 107 standard. Higher classes have more bright background and reflective material and more body coverage. Class 1 is for lower-risk settings away from traffic. Class 2 is the common choice near moving traffic. Class 3 adds more coverage, including sleeves, for higher-risk work.

Class 2 vs Class 3

The practical difference is coverage and visibility. Class 3 garments cover more of the body and are meant to be seen from farther away and in more conditions. Check the current standard text for exact material amounts.

How to find out which class you need

  • Ask the client, general contractor or site owner what their safety plan requires.
  • Check your state transportation rules if you work near public roads.
  • Check OSHA and your insurer for requirements that apply to your work.

Common questions

What is a Class 2 safety vest?

A high visibility vest with a defined amount of bright background and reflective material, commonly used near moving traffic. Check the ANSI/ISEA 107 standard for details.

What is the difference between ANSI Class 2 and 3?

Class 3 has more visible material and covers more of the body, including sleeves, for higher-risk work.
